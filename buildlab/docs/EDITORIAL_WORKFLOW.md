# Engineering Journal workflow

The Journal displays proposed topics and any approved published articles. Drafts remain hidden. For the step-by-step editing and deployment guide, see [JOURNAL_PUBLISHING.md](./JOURNAL_PUBLISHING.md). Publish only work based on real team experience.

## Proposed cadence

Aim for one reviewed engineering article, one project update or case study, and one mentorship or developer experience article each month when there is enough verified material. Consider a quarterly technical session only after a speaker and topic are confirmed. This is an editorial target, not a public promise.

## Article record

Before publication, record the title, category, real author, their contribution, draft date, publication date, technical reviewer, review status, and approved links or images. Journal cards use local SVG covers while topics are proposals. When an approved article has a real image, replace its cover image with an optimized local WebP, AVIF, or JPEG at the same aspect ratio. Keep `width` and `height`, use `loading="lazy"` below the first card, and write descriptive alt text for meaningful images. Do not use third-party image URLs or unapproved client screenshots. Suggested categories: Engineering Notes, Team Culture, Engineering Practice, and Mentorship.

## Review sequence

1. Author drafts from first-hand work or interviews with contributors.
2. Technical reviewer checks facts, code examples, security, and attribution.
3. Project owner checks client confidentiality and publication rights.
4. Editor checks clarity, accessibility, links, and metadata.
5. Publish only after review approval; update the article date when materially revised.

## Initial topic queue

- Lessons from Building a Multi-Tenant SaaS Platform — publish only if the team has direct, approved experience to discuss.
- What Makes a Good Code Review?
- From Local Development to Production — publish only with verified deployment examples.
- From Learning Syntax to Shipping Software.
- Architecture Decisions Behind Our SaaS Platforms — requires documented decisions and permission.
- Your First Contribution to a Team Codebase.
- Working With Git, Issues, and Pull Requests.
- How We Take Software From Idea to Deployment.

These are ideas, not evidence that the articles or underlying projects already exist.
